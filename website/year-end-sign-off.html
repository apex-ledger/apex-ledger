Sign in

Every check green. Then sign and lock.

Year-End Sign-off is the clipboard an accountant runs before closing a period: one row per check, a traffic light beside each, the reason when it is not green, and a click straight to the report or screen that fixes it.

What is checked

  • Books balance: trial balance balances; assets equal liabilities plus equity.
  • Bank and cash: no bank or cash account below zero; Undeposited Funds cleared; suspense and clearing accounts at zero.
  • Sub-ledgers: receivables and payables agree with open invoices and bills; no stock item below zero.
  • Sales tax: no GST/HST line still waiting for an amount; returns filed for the period.
  • Payroll: every pay run posted; source deductions remitted to the CRA.
  • Housekeeping: no journal entries left in draft; no quick entries dated before the period; fiscal periods locked through the period end.
  • Audit tests: the Audit Exceptions report run over the period: duplicates, round amounts, weekend postings, backdated entries.

How it works

  • Pick the period. Each check shows green (pass), amber (review) or red (fix), with the reason in plain words.
  • Open any red or amber row and it takes you to the report or entry concerned. Correct it, come back, and watch the light change.
  • When every check is green, the decision reads "ready". Enter the reviewer's name and a note, sign, and the period locks so nothing dated inside it can be posted.
  • Every sign-off is kept: who signed, when, what the lights said, and the note. A period can be signed again after a correction, and the history stays.

What it pairs with

  • Working papers, adjusting entries and the GIFI export for the T2, or the T2125 for a sole proprietor.
  • The CPA review workflow, where a preparer flags and a reviewer clears, and the month-end close checklist that runs the same way twelve times a year.
  • Client CRM deadlines, so the sign-off date lands on the client's record.
